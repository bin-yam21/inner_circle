Tilt is trading in an emotional state after losses: bigger size, faster entries, abandoned rules. It is the most common way accounts blow up.

## What ICT teaches
- **Halve the risk after a loss.** A 2% loss means the next trade risks 1%, and the one after a further loss risks 0.5%. "Equity preservation is the number one rule."
- With half size, **2R recovers the loss**. You don't need bigger risk; "you do it by scaling back your risk."
- Any loss "could be the beginning of a 10-trade losing streak". Increasing size is "throwing good money after bad" and builds fear-based trading.
- Late in the week, after recovering a loss: **take it off, close the week flat**, and don't carry a loss into the weekend.

## Warning signs of tilt
- Entering within minutes of a stop-out.
- Moving or removing stops.
- Trading outside your killzones or your pairs.
- Thinking "I need to make it back today".

## Practical tools
- The **Tilt guard** on the Psychology page reads your journal. After two losses in a row it reminds you to halve your risk. After three, or after you hit your daily loss limit, it tells you to stop for the day.
- Set a **daily loss limit** (for example 2R) and a **cool-down** of 30 minutes after any loss.

## Exercises
1. Set your daily loss limit in **Psychology → Settings for tilt guard**.
2. Review your journal for any trade taken within 30 minutes of a loss, and compare its R with your average.
