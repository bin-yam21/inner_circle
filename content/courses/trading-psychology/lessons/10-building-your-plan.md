Your plan has to fit **you**: your patience, your schedule, your life.

## What ICT teaches
- "It's never been my goal to make you a copy of ICT." Your bread-and-butter setup should be the one you see most easily.
- He trades only **three setups**: inside the range, order blocks, and turtle soups. "I could reduce it to one."
- Match the timeframe to your life. Monthly/weekly suits people with jobs; the daily chart is the best all-rounder; 4-hour and below is day trading.
- The mentorship's end goal is a **flowchart**: at every decision point you know what to do, or that you should wait.

## Plan template
1. **Markets:** 1–3 pairs.
2. **Style and timeframes:** for example short-term trading with a daily bias and 1-hour entries.
3. **Setups:** at most two, with exact entry, stop and target rules.
4. **Time:** which killzones you trade, and which news events you avoid.
5. **Risk:** % per trade, the halving rule, daily and weekly loss limits, the drawdown protocol.
6. **Routine:** check-in, journal, weekly review.
7. **Go-live criteria:** numbers from your demo journal.

## Exercises
1. Fill in the plan template in **My rules** (one rule per line).
2. Re-read it every Monday, and change it only during the weekend review, never mid-session.
