A single trade's result says almost nothing about your skill. A good process can lose, and a bad process can win. Over many trades, though, the process decides the equity curve.

## What ICT teaches
- Trading must be **process-oriented and binary**: "do X or do Y". Without a written process, "emotional, psychological and impulsive trading creeps in."
- A high-reward setup needs **seven things in agreement**. Your job is to check them, not to predict.
- "You want your trading to be boring… mundane and routine." Excitement belongs to the weekend review, not the trading week.

## Why it works
- **Variance.** Even a strong edge loses often. At a 40% win rate, 5 losses in a row is normal over a year of trading.
- **Judging decisions, not results.** A trade that followed every rule and lost is a *good trade*. A trade that broke rules and won is a *bad trade* that teaches a dangerous lesson.

## Exercises
1. In **Journal**, add a checklist score to every trade. After 20 trades, compare your results at 8+/10 versus below 8.
2. Write three rules on the **Psychology → My rules** page that define a valid setup for you.
3. After each session, grade yourself on *process* in the Daily log, separately from profit and loss.
