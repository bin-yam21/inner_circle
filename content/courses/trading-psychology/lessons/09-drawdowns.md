Every trader has drawdowns. What matters is keeping them small and responding with rules, not emotion.

## What ICT teaches
- Target **≤15% annual drawdown**; 20% is tolerable; above 25% scares investors.
- Allocate only **30% of equity** to trading and calculate risk on that slice.
- Reduce size as losses accumulate. A drawdown is a signal to *trade smaller and more selectively*, not harder.
- One trade doesn't have to erase all losses: "It doesn't have to come back in all one trade."

## A drawdown protocol
1. **−3R:** halve risk and trade only A+ setups (8+/10 checklist).
2. **−6R:** stop live trading and go back to demo for a week. Review every trade.
3. **−10R:** full review: is the market regime different (ICT's quarterly shift)? Are you breaking rules? Is the strategy still valid?
