The journal is your mirror. It turns vague feelings into data you can act on.

## What ICT teaches
- From Month 1: keep a **daily price-action log**. Note each day's high and low and when they formed, plus fast moves, untested highs and lows, and equal highs and lows. "Even after 23 years of trading I still do this… it's what professionals do."
- Keep your analysis chart separate from your execution chart, "so you don't marry your ideas".
- When you're wrong: "What did I see wrong? Take that as a big opportunity."

## What to log on every trade
1. Setup, timeframe, session, and checklist score.
2. Your emotions before and after (calm, fearful, greedy, rushed, bored).
3. Mistakes (chased, moved stop, oversized, outside killzone, no setup).
4. Screenshots before and after.
5. One lesson learned.

## Weekly review questions
- Which setup produced most of my R? Which cost me?
- Which mistakes repeated?
- Did I follow my rules on every trade? If not, what triggered the break?

## Exercises
1. Complete a **Weekly review** on the Psychology page every Saturday (ICT's study day).
2. Filter the Journal by mistake and remove your most expensive mistake for the next two weeks.
