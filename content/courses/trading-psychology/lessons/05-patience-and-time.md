Most trading time is waiting. The edge comes from waiting for price to reach your level at the right time, not from acting all the time.

## What ICT teaches
- "There are two elements in trading: you have to submit to **time** and **price**." "Time is a murderer for traders" if you won't accept that you don't know how long a setup takes.
- Print your chart markups, date them, and review at the weekend how long each level took to be reached. Hindsight examples hide the waiting.
- Higher-timeframe setups form slowly. Position traders may see only "two, maybe three" good setups a year.
- "Professional traders are not in a rush to put money at work."

## Practical tools
- **Alerts instead of staring.** Mark the level, set a price alert, and walk away (Orderblocks lesson).
- **Killzone-only trading.** Restrict yourself to London or New York windows. Outside them, you study.
- **The waiting log.** In your Daily log, write down "levels I'm waiting for" and check them off when they're hit.

## Exercises
1. For one week, write your expected levels every morning in the **Daily log**. On Friday, count how many were reached and how long they took.
2. Use **Tools → IPDA calculator** to find the 20/40/60-day dates for your pair, and note when the next setup window opens.
