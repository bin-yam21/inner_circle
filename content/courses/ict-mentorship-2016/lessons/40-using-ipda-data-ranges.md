- First a study habit: print or save every forum chart ICT marks up, date it, keep a binder, and review at the weekend HOW LONG each level took to be reached. Hindsight examples hide the "submission to time" you must learn.
- Use the FUTURES contract alongside FX: it gives real volume and OPEN INTEREST that spot FX doesn't.
- IPDA data ranges do NOT call highs/lows exactly on day 20/40/60 (sometimes they do). Their job: tell you WHICH highs/lows/gaps the algorithm will reference.
- Steps: (1) Find the most OBVIOUS quarterly market structure shift in the last ~3 months (tip: divide the daily chart into quarters with lines at Mar/Jun/Sep/Dec). AUD futures: the Nov 2016 shift (false break above 0.7700 equal highs → structure broke lower). (2) Roll back to the FIRST day of that month. (3) Look back 20/40/60 days: highest high (buy stops), lowest low (sell stops), FVGs, voids, equilibrium of consolidations, OTE. (4) Cast forward 20/40/60 days for the window of the next significant move.
- If ALL highs and lows inside the 60-day look-back have been run ("both sides cleared"), price must expand to the next high or low OUTSIDE the 60 days → expect a big, explosive move. Combine with weekly/monthly to see which one.
- The algorithm brings price NEAR the level; then the 10–20 pip spike beyond it is bank/broker-level execution. Banks don't see retail; they see fund-level pending orders.
- AUD example: 40 days after 1 Nov 2016, price hit ~0.7150 (a mid-figure level and an old weekly low from Feb/May 2016 where the weekly bullish OB sat) and turned up — a quarterly shift.
- OPEN INTEREST (from Larry Williams' "How I Made One Million Dollars… Trading Commodities"): view OI as the central bank/liquidity provider holding the SELL side for buyers. OI peaks during the Aug–Oct 0.7450–0.7700 consolidation = their sell program; OI falling during the decline = short covering; a ≥15% OI COLLAPSE at major support (Dec 2016, 0.7150) = they've finished selling and reset → bullish. Rising OI before a turtle-soup sell confirms the sell.
- After the turn: from the new 40-day marker, look back 20 days → the mid-December equal highs (buy stops) = the target. New daily bullish OBs held (0.7355 tested, low 0.7352, only 3 pips through); the move filled the Dec 2016 FVG 0.7380–0.7434 and passed its mean threshold → targets 0.7525, 0.7570–0.7580, maybe 0.7650 (weekly bearish OB from late Oct 2016).
- Retail saw a "double top at the 50% Fib" and sold; ICT was buying.
- Consolidation near the 20/40/60-day equilibrium with no news = likely a sideways day.
- Homework: on any market, put a line on the first trading day of each month, mark the 60/40/20-day highs and lows, and watch whether they get run. ICT says this is how he first "cracked" it (on a Swiss franc chart, then corn, soybeans, cattle, metals). Do the same comparison for NZD, EUR and GBP.
- Hedging and quarterly resets: liquidity providers carry real risk (e.g. 9/11, Brexit, the Trump election) and "pay themselves" with the quarterly shifts.
- ICT: his background is a computer-science degree (information systems), which made him see price delivery as a program working from data ranges.
