- Liquidity = open interest of buyers and sellers, concentrated near specific price levels. Retail buys/sells at market; smart money sells ABOVE market (to buy stops/breakout buyers) and buys BELOW market (from sell stops) → always selling at a premium, buying at a discount.
- Role-play: "If I were short, where would my protective buy stop be? If long, where's my sell stop?" — you don't need order books or depth of market.
- First determine the higher-timeframe bias, then wait patiently: bullish → wait for a run below an old low (weak longs stopped out, sell-side liquidity injected) and buy; target buy stops above old highs, a bearish OB, FVG or void. Bearish → sell a run above an old high.
- Technique: in a bullish market, before price trades under the recent low, place a buy LIMIT just below it. On 15/30-minute charts expect a 10–20 pip sweep below the low; enter 10–20 pips under the low with a 30–50 pip stop. If it goes more than ~25 pips below the low, it's probably a real decline, not a stop run.
- Examples:
  - NZD/USD: sweep below an old low, exits at equal highs to the left.
  - USD/CAD: previous daily low 1.3102 (below the candle bodies); on rate-announcement day a buy limit at 1.3090 under the intraday low 1.3095; low printed 1.3083 (7 pips drawdown) → run to the swing-high buy stops at 1.3360 (~270 pips).
  - Dollar Index (Friday): buy 102.85/102.80 below Thursday's low (low 102.69) → ran equal-high buy stops, then the sell stops below relative equal lows; Fridays after a trending week tend to be choppy profit-taking.
  - USD/CHF: sweep below a low, layered buy-stop targets at 1.0125, 1.0130, 1.0145.
  - GBP/USD (16 Dec 2016 live session): sweep below the opening price/short-term low, then a run above 1.2475 toward 1.2500–1.2520.
