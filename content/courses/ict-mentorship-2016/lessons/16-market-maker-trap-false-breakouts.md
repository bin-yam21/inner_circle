- A false breakout ABOVE a consolidation shows up mainly in bearish markets; a false breakout BELOW a consolidation shows up in bullish markets.
- Neophyte and breakout traders bracket a range: buy stops above the highs, sell stops below the lows. Market makers push price to ONE side to neutralise those stops, depending on their model.
- Bullish model: price breaks below the consolidation → smart money buys those sell stops → expands to the buy stops above the old high to pair long exits (scale out). This repeats at each new consolidation, buying a little higher each time ("the orders stack higher").
- Example (Oct 2016, prices around 108.50–109.90 — a yen pair): stops taken below 108.55 → rally above 109.00; a run below 108.95 → target 109.40, swept just above the candle BODIES (bodies hold the volume); the larger range took 108.85 → 109.45–109.50 hit.
- Measured moves: the second leg equals the first (108.75 → 109.25); the projected 108.85 stop run gave 109.80, then 109.90, which was the daily bearish order block ICT had flagged live.
- Market makers aren't villains; they provide liquidity. The market always seeks the most recent untapped liquidity with the least resistance — that gives you direction. In a bullish profile, every drop below a consolidation = a false breakout = accumulation.
