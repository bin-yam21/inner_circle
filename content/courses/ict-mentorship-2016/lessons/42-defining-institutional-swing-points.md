- Simple swing high/low = 3-candle pattern (Larry Williams). But institutionally the market turns in only TWO ways: a STOP RUN (breaker swing point) or a FAILURE SWING. "I challenge you to show me anything else."
- BREAKER SWING POINT (the best, "most dynamic" pattern; ICT's favourite — a turtle soup): price rallies toward resistance (bearish OB, breaker, mitigation block, old high/low, gap) but stops just short; retail shorts with stops above the short-term high; price then drives above that high, takes the buy stops and fills the level, then breaks the short-term low between → market structure shift. Entry: sell right at the run above the old high (institutional style), OR wait and sell the return to the break point, stop above the high. The stops have already been run, so there's no reason for price to go back up there. Mirror for buys below an old low (the old low can be any age).
- Candlestick traders wait for the hammer/doji; institutional traders act while it's a "bold-faced candle" before the wick forms. On the daily you may need the whole day to confirm.
- FAILURE SWING: price trades into the level, breaks away, retests and fails to make another pass (no higher high / no lower low). You can't sell the high, so wait for the short-term swing in between to break, then sell the retracement to it with the stop just above the failed high (an area already "manipulated" — they won't let trapped traders out).
- Logic: institutions trap funds (Turtle-style breakout orders above 20-day highs / below 20-day lows) and reprice quickly so trapped traders must exit at worse prices.
- Practise selling old highs/buying old lows in a DEMO account: when right, feedback is immediate; when wrong, the stop is modest.
- Only two ways to sell: at an old high, or on a retracement to a market structure break. Only two ways to buy: at an old low, or on a return to the break. "There aren't 50,000 patterns."
