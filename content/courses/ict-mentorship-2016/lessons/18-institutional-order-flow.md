- Uses EUR/USD MONTHLY 2008–2012, then weekly and daily, to show how to read institutional order flow.
- Think like the market maker: where is the maximum liquidity relative to where price came from and where it is now? Wherever there are one-sided black (down) candles, there must later be green (up) candles to rebalance ("wherever there is a black candle there must be a green candle").
- CANDLE BODIES vs WICKS: the bodies carry the institutional volume (closest to interbank pricing); wicks are mostly retail pricing and broker spread expansion. Do the analysis around the bodies; expect price to run just below/above the BODIES to take stops (it doesn't need to clear the wicks). Mean threshold of an OB = middle of the body; bodies respect it, wicks overshoot.
- Monthly map: bearish OB (open ~1.5197, highs ~1.5143–1.5151) → institutional order flow BEARISH (red zones) until a monthly bullish OB and the sell stops below candle bodies are reached → flips BULLISH (blue zones) until the next monthly bearish OB/void. Repeating this maps the whole 2008–2012 cycle.
- On the weekly/daily inside a bearish monthly zone: sell bearish OBs, breakers and runs above old highs. The "unwinding" — the sell at a bearish OB undoes the longs smart money bought earlier; "everything is a hedge on the interbank level". This is the Market Maker SELL profile: consolidation → accumulation → smart money reversal → low-risk short → re-distribution → run below the consolidation.
- MITIGATION BLOCK: in a bullish phase, smart money sold the last up candle to drive price below lows (sell stops). Once price trades back above it, those shorts are underwater; when price returns into that candle's range (wick down to body), they cover and add new longs → explosive move. (Example low ~1.2151.)
- Daily, inside a bullish monthly zone: buy every down candle / bullish OB and propulsion candle (e.g. high 1.2802, low 1.2795; 1.3436 → 1.3423; 1.4626), up to the monthly bearish OB. Wait for a break of a short-term high before buying into the monthly bullish OB.
- Biggest moves come from monthly/weekly levels because that's where the "whales" (large funds) have their stops — banks can't counterparty with retail, only with funds. Transpose monthly/weekly levels onto the daily chart and you'll see major swings coming.
