- Definition: a range in price delivery where only ONE side of liquidity was offered, usually confirmed by a liquidity void on lower timeframes; price can also literally gap.
- How ICT frames it (EUR/USD daily, Dec 2016): take the big down candle. The candle to its LEFT had buy-side liquidity offered from its low up to its close (it came off its low); the candle to its RIGHT offered buy-side liquidity from its open to its high. What's left between the left candle's LOW and the right candle's HIGH is the FVG — sell-side only. Here ~1.0500 down to 1.0475 (~25 pips).
- A daily FVG usually shows up as a liquidity void (several candles) on lower timeframes.
- 4-hour follow-through: price ran an old low (sell stops; turtle soup long), then rallied toward equal highs (buy stops) sitting just below the FVG and filled it — over 100 pips in about two days.
- Range-bound conditions (e.g. end-of-year holiday trading) = trade stop runs and FVG fills.
- Overlap of concepts (5- and 15-minute, 19 Dec 2016): the second run to 1.0475 took the old high (1.0477 → high 1.0478 = liquidity pool) AND filled the FVG; then a 2-pip gap between candle bodies (close 1.0472 → next open 1.0470) → sell limit at 1.0470. The up candle's body closed exactly into that gap = "efficient price delivery". Later a further sell at 1.0455/1.0450 targeting sell stops below 1.0415/1.0410.
- "Perfect delivery of price" = every range has been offered both on the way down and on the way up.
