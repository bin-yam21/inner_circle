- Daily charts only; a 3–6 month outlook. ICT ignores bank research reports ("why would a bank tell you its plays?") and doesn't sift fundamental data: he reads the interest-rate markets visually.
- Quarterly shift: every 3–4 months the prevailing trend reverses or enters an extended consolidation.
- Bond prices move opposite to yields: bonds falling = rates rising = supportive of the US dollar. 30-year T-bond = mortgage benchmark; also watch the 10-year note.
- SMT / interest-rate divergence: last week of June 2016 — DXY failed to make a lower low while T-bonds made a higher high → dollar turn. Bonds then weakened (rates up) while DXY retraced into a bullish OB; bonds rallied into a bearish OB and sold off again → dollar rally accelerated (target 101–102).
- 10-year vs 30-year SMT, Sept 2016: 10-year made a higher high while the 30-year made a lower high (2nd week and last week of September) → both then fell (rates up) → dollar rallies in those weeks: USD/CHF, USD/CAD, USD/JPY up; EUR, GBP, NZD, AUD down. Supported ICT's GBP target of 1.20.
- US election (Nov 2016): overnight the dollar index and Dow futures sank (Dow futures −750 points), but the debt instruments did not confirm (no higher high in 10-/30-year futures) → "fluff/manipulation"; DXY then rallied 10 days in a row as bonds sold off.
- "Interest rates control everything"; German bunds were falling too.
