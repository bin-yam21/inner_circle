- Practise position trading for at least a year. Keep annual drawdown ≤ 15% (20% OK; &gt; 25% makes investors cringe). A steady equity curve attracts investors (managed money / prop firms).
- ALLOCATE ONLY 30% OF EQUITY to trading: risk is a % of that 30%, not of the full balance (e.g. $10,000 → trade as $3,000 → 1% risk = $30). No margin calls, no wild equity swings, cash reserve for unexpected setups. Max 1% (or 2%) of the 30%.
- Target 3:1+ on higher timeframes; low risk + high reward tolerates low accuracy, but needs patience.
- Realistic annual return: 18–25% (managed-fund standard; 16% on $10 million is "respectable"). Funds earn a 2% management fee plus performance bonus; keep trade frequency LOW (each trade exposes clients to risk).
- Only about 2 (sometimes 3, rarely 4) good position-trade setups a year.
- You must sit through give-backs of open profit. Counter them with short-term trades in correlated/inverse pairs (e.g. short USD/JPY long-term → short EUR/USD or GBP/USD during the USD/JPY bounce), since US traders can't hedge.
- STOP SIZE IS NOT A MEASURE OF SKILL: ICT's intraday max is ~30–35 pips (about a third of a ~100-pip ADR); position trades may need ~200-pip stops (aim 600 pips for 3:1).
- Don't rush stops to break-even on position trades. Take partials at logical resistance and add back (even slightly more) on the retracement.
- "Long term is not get-rich-quick but get-rich-steady." Day trading gives "velocity" (money turns over quickly); position trading doesn't.
- Homework: complete at least one long-term trade held for 3+ months.
