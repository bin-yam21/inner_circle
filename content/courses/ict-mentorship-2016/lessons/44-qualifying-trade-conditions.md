- Builds on the seasonal tendency: the 10-year T-note tends to rally (make a low) in June.
- How to qualify it: compare swings in the 10-year note with the Dollar Index. Perfect symmetry is a mirror image (note lower lows ↔ DXY higher highs). When symmetry breaks ("crack in correlation" = SMT divergence), an underlying trade is under way.
- June 2015: note made 5–6 lower lows while DXY made LOWER HIGHS (not higher highs) → crack in correlation; yields were falling while note futures rallied.
- June 2016: note made equal lows while DXY made higher highs → crack in correlation → qualifying condition.
- Nov 2016 (March 2017 note contract): note made a lower high (ignore the election wick) while DXY made a lower low instead of the expected mirror → dollar rallied for two months; open interest declined (smart money short covering) and the 10-year yield rose. "Markets seek yield": rising yields → dollar tends to rally.
- Combine with quarterly shifts: ICT's horizon is ~3 months; most trades complete in about half that time.
- Qualifiers (one is enough): seasonal + SMT divergence between DXY and the 10-year note, an interest-rate triad, or SMT between a forex pair and DXY. Even day traders and scalpers should align with this higher-timeframe order flow.
