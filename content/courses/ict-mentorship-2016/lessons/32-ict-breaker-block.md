- Bullish breaker: price makes a short-term low, then a lower low that runs sell stops (a "false break / turtle soup"). Then price rallies through the short-term HIGH between the two lows = market structure shift. That high (the last up-close candle(s) in the swing high before the old low was violated — use the whole range of the highest up candle) becomes support.
- Why it works: the sellers who drove price below the old low are underwater; they mitigate (close) shorts at breakeven and add longs when price returns to the swing high.
- Bearish breaker: the down-close candle in the most recent swing low before an old high is violated. Price runs buy stops above the old high, buyers are trapped long, price reprices lower and breaks the swing low; a return to that swing low is a sell.
- Telltale signs: (1) raid on stops beyond old high/low, (2) range expansion that takes out the short-term swing between, (3) quick repricing.
- ICT's point: horizontal support/resistance lines alone fail; you need the "storyline" (liquidity run → mitigation).
