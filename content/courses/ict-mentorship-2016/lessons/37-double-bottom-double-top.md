- Retail sees a double top as resistance (shorts with buy stops above) and a double bottom as support. ICT: never trust them — the interbank algorithm runs through old highs/lows to reach the stops.
- Map both sides: above the double top there may be a liquidity void / bearish OB (e.g. last up candles opening at 77.42); below, a fair value gap and the bullish OB (last down candle before the up move). Scenario: price drops into the FVG/bullish OB, then rallies through the double top, fills the void and hits 77.42 to the pip, then runs the sell-side liquidity.
- MEASURED MOVE: measure from the double-top high down to the low between the two peaks and project it above the highs → the algorithm's objective. Example: projected 0.7445, actual high 0.7446 (one pip off), with a "minor divergence" on a momentum oscillator at the old resistance that would fool retail into selling. Same projection works below a double bottom (a 48-pip expansion on the hourly).
- Intraday stop runs are usually 10–20 pips beyond a high/low on the 15-minute; on hourly charts and above use the double-top/bottom range projection instead.
- Extremes of the range = high probability; the middle = low probability. Mark clean equal highs/lows on all timeframes. After buy stops are taken, look for the sell stops next.
