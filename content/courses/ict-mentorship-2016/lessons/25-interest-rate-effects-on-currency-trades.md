- Interest rates are "the single most influential driving force behind market moves".
- Accumulation/distribution through comparison: in BEARISH conditions a benchmark (Dow for stocks, DXY for currencies, CRB for commodities) makes higher highs while closely correlated assets make LOWER highs → smart money distribution. BULLISH: benchmark makes lower lows while correlated assets make HIGHER lows → accumulation (heavy buying won't let price go to a discount).
- INTEREST RATE TRIAD: 30-year T-bond (long-term), 10-year note (intermediate), 5-year note (short-term) futures (free charts at barchart.com). They should confirm each other's highs/lows at the moment DXY is at a significant level. A failure swing in ANY ONE of them = interest-rate shift / smart money → validates the DXY setup.
- Example (week of 8 Dec 2016): DXY dipped to 99.50 into an old bullish OB from mid-November and rallied hard all week. 90-minute charts 5–8 Dec: 30-year made a LOWER high, 10-year flat, 5-year a higher high → divergence → dollar long validated. EUR/USD rallied above 1.0815–1.0820 to clear buy stops and reached the old low at 1.0865 → short; cable failed to make a higher high when EUR/USD did (another SMT).
- Action plan: when price hits a focus point (order block, liquidity pool, liquidity void, FVG), check the triad and DXY. If there is no sign of smart money, pass on the trade. Rate futures going up = rates falling = bearish dollar, and vice versa.
