- Retail draws imaginary diagonal lines (higher lows → support; lower highs → resistance) and buys or sells the "third touch". ICT: in nearly 24 years he found no statistical edge in diagonal trend lines; it's subjective, a "coin flip". "Price has no awareness of your trend line"; banks don't value them. Price only respects real liquidity (stops and pending orders), mostly at the FUND level (funds get cannibalised at reversals).
- Trap on an UP-trend line (when higher timeframes are not bullish): aim to SELL at the swing high between touch 2 and touch 3 — at a bearish OB, a lower-timeframe bearish breaker, or a brief poke above that high (turtle soup). Expect price to fall through the line and run the stops below touch 2.
- Trap on a DOWN-trend line (HTF bullish): BUY at the low between touch 2 and 3 — bullish OB or turtle soup below it; target the buy stops above the touch-2 high.
- GBP/USD, 11 Dec 2015: price hit a daily bearish level at 1.5234 (filled a daily FVG). The 15-/30-minute up-trend line drew retail buyers, but each "touch" only returned to bearish OBs/mean thresholds → selloff through the line to the daily target 1.5060.
- GBP/USD (May 2016): daily bullish OB at 1.4385 (two down candles); a 15-minute down-trend line + "triangle" — the third touch was a BUY at 1.4385–1.4390, running the buy stops above the second high. Another daily bullish OB (open 1.4446, mean threshold ~1.4400, 18 May 2016) → rally to the 1.4600 big figure (last up candle on the daily; price must rebalance the one-sided delivery).
- Use retail "fingerprints" (obvious lines) as the reason to do the opposite, justified by daily institutional reference points.
