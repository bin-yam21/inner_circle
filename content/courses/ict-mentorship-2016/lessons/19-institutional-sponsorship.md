- Institutional sponsorship = the willingness of large institutions to PROTECT an underlying price swing that's likely to unfold. Criteria for LONGS:
- 1. HIGHER-TIMEFRAME PRICE DISPLACEMENT (reversal, expansion, or return to fair value) — "the elephant stepped into the kiddie pool" and the water overflowed.
- 2. An intermediate-term imbalance: a move to DISCOUNT or a sell-side liquidity run (below an old low).
- 3. Short-term BUY-side liquidity above the market to pair long exits with.
- 4. TIME-OF-DAY influence: the London-open low of the day or a New York session low.
- (Shorts: mirror — displacement, move to premium/buy-stop raid, sell-side liquidity below, London-open or New York high.)
- USD/JPY daily example: a run below an old low (sell stops = longs' protection, not new shorts) → a dynamic surge = displacement → the ORIGIN of that move is the bullish OB (body of the last down candle) → price came back to it and was bought again → targets: short-term highs, a liquidity void, equal highs, old swing high.
- The key test: sponsored levels react IMMEDIATELY. If price is lethargic after entry, reduce risk, halve the position or exit; you can re-enter later. Losers should be quick.
- Grade the swing from origin → first stage → equilibrium → terminus; sponsorship should stop price from coming back to the origin once structure has broken higher.
- 4-hour/60-minute detail, the "super secret sauce": the NEW YORK MIDNIGHT OPENING PRICE. In a bullish move, each day price dips BELOW the midnight open into a bullish OB/void (London or New York) and is bought → higher close each day. This is POWER OF 3: on a bullish day buy near or below the open, exit near the close.
- Retail sells old highs as "resistance"; price consolidates there, runs their buy stops, retraces to an earlier OB (re-capitalised), then continues. A late retracement gets everyone short ("short USD/JPY on the forums") just before the final push to the old high = terminus.
- Successful trades show the hallmark: re-capitalised OBs from previous London/New York sessions and moves gravitating toward higher-timeframe buy-stop pools.
