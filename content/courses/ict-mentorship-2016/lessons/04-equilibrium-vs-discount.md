- Origin of OTE (introduced by ICT in 2010). The Fibonacci has no magic; it only makes equilibrium (50% of the current range) visible.
- Buying needs MOVEMENT first: an IMPULSIVE PRICE SWING = displacement by someone with deep pockets (banks let price go higher only when it suits them). Draw the Fib from the swing low to the swing high of the impulse (the parent swing = the largest current range).
- Wait for a SWING HIGH (high candle with a lower candle on each side) and a FOURTH candle lower → only then wait for price to come down to equilibrium (50% = fair market value, neutral-to-bullish). Don't count Sunday candles.
- BELOW equilibrium = DISCOUNT; the highest-probability bullish zone is the OTE 62%–70.5%–79%. In a bullish market, price shouldn't stay in discount long — expect an explosive move away. The impulse's starting low must not break (stop below it).
- Then hunt entries on lower timeframes (4-hour, 60, 15, 5-minute) using OBs, mitigation blocks, breakers, turtle soups, OTE. If price breaks below the OTE and takes an old low, expect a TURTLE SOUP long (stop run) — "how do I know if it will keep falling?" starts here.
- Exits: above old highs/equal highs (buy stops), or at a bearish OB (open 0.9768, high 0.9768 exactly on USD/CHF); exit just BEFORE the level. Smart money distributes longs above old highs; it doesn't need the oldest high.
- Intraday stop runs reach 10–20 pips above a high; equal highs are "too clean".
- USD/CHF daily (Aug–Sept 2016) examples: buy ~0.9550 → equal highs ~0.9850 (~300 pips); 140/272/400-pip runs; a 175-pip run into a bearish OB. Some trades fail ("you're going to lose sometimes"); about one good daily setup per week.
- Hourly works the same: consolidation at equilibrium → expansion to liquidity (buy stops above highs).
- Indicators (Fib, divergence, oscillators) only "work" when price-action foundations agree. ICT dislikes USD/CHF and JPY ("sneaky pairs").
