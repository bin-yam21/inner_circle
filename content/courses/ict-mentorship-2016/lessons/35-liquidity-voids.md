- Liquidity void = a range where price was delivered on only ONE side: long, one-sided candles (often a small gap between the two biggest candles). Price usually wants to revisit and fill that "porous" range.
- Consolidation = price in balance (equilibrium). When price leaves it aggressively, that's smart money (only they can move price) → imbalance / displacement.
- No set time limit: voids can fill within a session or stay open for months; judge by surrounding price action.
- A drop like this is a void of BUY-side liquidity (almost no buying on the way down), so expect price to trade back up over the same levels.
- Example (1-, 5- and 15-minute charts, 15–19 Dec 2016): consolidation near the 104.80 institutional level, void from 104.76 down to ~104.50/104.30. Sell stops built under short-term lows (104.10/104.08) were run first (smart money pairs those sell stops with its buys), then a ~75-pip rally from ~104.05 filled the void to 104.76.
- Price hit 104.76 twice (16 and 19 Dec); after the second test, two bearish candles appeared with a small gap between bodies = a common gap. Sell limit at 104.70 inside the gap → very little drawdown, then runs on sell stops at 104.04 (cover) and 103.65.
- Institutional pricing happens in graduated steps — it can't all be done in one pass; repeated tests of a level after an aggressive move away = more selling being added.
