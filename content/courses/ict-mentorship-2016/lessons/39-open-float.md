- Open float = the current open interest of pending orders above and below price: buy stops (shorts' protection, breakout longs) above highs; sell stops below lows.
- Buy-side liquidity lists: above the last bearish shift, above the weekly/monthly high, above the 3-MONTH high (the key one for quarterly shifts), the 6-month high and the 12-month high (you can sit near a 12-month high for a long time). Mirror for sell-side.
- EUR/USD daily example (Dec 2015 – Dec 2016), with quarterly markers:
  - After the bearish shift to the 1.0534 low (Dec 2015), your eyes go to the high it came from: fund buy stops above it.
  - A bullish structure shift → retracement → fill of the October void → retrace to 1.0860 → run on buy stops above 1.1500 (~1.1570s, May 2016). From 1.0534 to 1.1500, sell stops were barely touched — the market was working the BUY side.
  - After the 1.1500 raid: a break of a short-term low (first clue it's turning to the sell side). Each later rally only poked above an insignificant short-term high, failed (e.g. near 1.1400, 1.1295 on the US-election spike), and each low broke further → lower intermediate-term highs → the market wanted the open float BELOW. It filled a void around 1.0680–1.0635 and broke 1.0700 toward the 1.0534 low (Dec 2016).
- Swing hierarchy (from Larry Williams): an intermediate-term high has a lower short-term high on each side; a long-term high has lower intermediate-term highs on each side (like a head-and-shoulders top). Mirror for lows.
- Moves of ~3–6 months (Dec 2015 → May 2016 → Dec 2016) = quarterly shifts in action.
- How do you know if a run above a high will reverse? Watch which side keeps gaining ground: if rallies only take short-term highs without new ground and each drop makes new lows, the market seeks the sell side — and vice versa. This gives the daily bias for all styles of trading.
