- Indicators measure the past; "price has no awareness of our indicators". ICT used them from 1992 and had false hope in them. Banks (UBS, Credit Suisse, Citi) don't look at stochastics — they target stops.
- Type 1 (classic) divergence: higher high in price, lower high in momentum = bearish; lower low in price, higher low in momentum = bullish. Type 2 (hidden / trend-following, credited to Nick Van Nuys, not George Lane): higher low in price while momentum makes a lower low = bullish continuation.
- A "fifth condition": is it even time to trade? In consolidation, don't.
- Use indicators only to see what RETAIL is thinking, then look for the institutional reason the opposite should happen.
- USD/CAD 1-hour, 10–11 Nov 2016: higher high + bearish divergence (retail sells). But equal highs/buy stops sat just above, and below were equal lows plus a bullish OB. Price dipped to clear the equal lows into the OB's mean threshold (hidden bullish divergence), then rallied through the highs.
- USD/CHF 1-hour: bearish divergence after a rally; retail targets old lows. ICT expected a drop only into the last down candle's BODY (~0.9645–0.9640) without breaking its middle → long 0.9645, exit ~0.9708; the low printed exactly 0.9645, then price ran through 0.9728 and the equal highs at 0.9760. Repeated "divergence" sell signals kept failing in the uptrend.
