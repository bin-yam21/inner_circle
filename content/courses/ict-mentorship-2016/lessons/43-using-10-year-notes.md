- 10-year T-note seasonal: high in Jan–Feb → LOW late May/June–early July → rally into December (bearish H1, bullish H2). Charts: futures prices on barchart.com, yields on investing.com. Price is inverse to yield. "Long-term funds seek yield."
- DXY seasonal: rallies Jan–Feb into March; significant HIGH June–July, then declines into the last week of Oct/first week of Nov, small bounce in November. In bull markets buy the dollar in Nov, Jan and May; sells come Mar, Jun–Jul and Sept.
- Normal relationship: notes rally (yields fall) → dollar falls.
- CONDITION CHECK: if the 10-year and DXY move in the SAME direction → long-term indecision → big consolidation → trade stop runs back to the middle of the range, short-term trades and day trades only (2015 and mid-2016: notes and DXY both rallied; EUR and GBP were range-bound until Brexit).
- If the 10-year moves with its seasonal and DXY moves OPPOSITE → trending environment → position trades for weeks or months (where the big funds put money). Example: March 2017 note contract made its seasonal high in Nov 2016 (election) → notes fell, yields rose, the dollar trended higher into December.
- If the strongest seasonal buy (June–July) fails, trade the opposite — be a bearish note trader into the seasonal highs.
