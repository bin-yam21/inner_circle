- Removes ambiguity about "which level matters" with a HIERARCHY of PD arrays on monthly, weekly and daily charts (nothing below daily for higher-timeframe analysis).
- Define the current range from the old high to the old low; the midpoint = equilibrium. Above = premium; below = discount.
- PREMIUM arrays, in the order price meets them going UP from equilibrium: (1) mitigation block → (2) bearish breaker → (3) liquidity void → (4) fair value gap → (5) bearish order block → (6) rejection block (just above the candle bodies) → (7) old high / old low (the deepest premium; an old long-term LOW can act as resistance too).
- DISCOUNT arrays going DOWN: bullish mitigation block → bullish breaker → liquidity void → FVG → bullish order block → rejection block (just below the lowest bodies) → old low / old high.
- BREAKERS take precedence: if there's a breaker, price probably won't go beyond it — expect the void/gap/OB beyond it to stay untouched.
- Mitigation block = a failure swing's short-term swing low (in a move down): longs who failed to make another pass at the high are underwater and sell to get out. Small bounces in bear markets are mitigation blocks.
- Process: at equilibrium, scan the LEFT side of the chart for each array in order; if one isn't present, "check it off" and look for the next. Bearish OB is almost always present.
- Price moves premium ↔ discount until a major event creates a one-sided imbalance. Price never goes in a straight line.
- Time and price: you must submit to an unknown amount of TIME ("time is a murderer for traders") while studying PRICE for confirmation; profit comes when both agree.
