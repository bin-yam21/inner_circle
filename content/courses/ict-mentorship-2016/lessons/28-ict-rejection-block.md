- First anticipation skill: every time a new high or low forms, expect some rejection. Turtle soup = false break above an old high (turtle soup sell) / below an old low (turtle soup long).
- Price doesn't need a higher high for a failure swing: look at the candle BODIES.
- Bearish rejection block: best in major/intermediate downtrends. A swing high with long wick(s), possibly over several candles. Frame it from the highest wick high down to the highest open or close (body) in the swing high — color doesn't matter. Treat the zone like a bearish order block.
- Entry: (a) aggressive — sell when price trades back up to the block's low (highest body level), stop above the wick high; or (b) wait for price to trade above the body level without making a new wick high, then sell on a STOP below it — selling on weakness (one of the few times ICT uses a stop entry).
- Classic chartists may call the formation a bull flag, but price moves on orders, not patterns; this is distribution.
- Bullish rejection block: in uptrends; swing low with wick(s); framed from the lowest wick low up to the lowest open/close; buy when price trades back down to the block's high.
- Also use it for exits: cover shorts just below the lowest open/close of a previous swing low; don't demand that price reach the wicks. "The bodies are the closest thing to institutional understanding" on retail charts.
