- Seasonal charts: 40-year average (blue, since 1976) vs 15-year average (red) of futures prices by calendar month; currency futures deliver in March, June, September and December. Little difference between the two lines → reliable tendency. A roadmap, not a guarantee — one more confluence.
- Canadian dollar futures tend to RALLY mid-March → May/June and DECLINE September → just before Christmas.
- FX inversion: when USD is the base currency (USD/CAD, USD/JPY, USD/CHF), flip the futures seasonal. CAD weakness Sept–Dec = USD/CAD bullish.
- USD/CAD weekly 2008–2016: strong Sept–Dec rallies in 2008, 2011, 2012, 2013, 2014, 2015, 2016; weak in 2009–2010 when the weekly trend was bearish — only use the seasonal when the market is already predisposed (institutional order flow agrees).
- Crude oil (Canada's #1 export) tends to rally about mid-Feb/March → June; visible even during the 2014–2016 bear market (from ~105). Oil and CAD move almost together.
- Combine with quarterly shifts: the shift tells you a move is due every 3–4 months; seasonals suggest which direction for that quarter.
- ICT lost money early by trading every seasonal with too much size; only later combined them with quarterly shifts.
