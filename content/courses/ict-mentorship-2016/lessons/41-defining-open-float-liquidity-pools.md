- Large funds keep buy stops above old highs and sell stops below old lows; on an intermediate (3–4 month) basis the market targets this fund-level liquidity every quarter.
- OPEN FLOAT: from any reference date, look BACK 60 trading days and cast FORWARD 60 trading days (120 days total). The highest high / lowest low inside = where the large-fund buy/sell stops are.
- Use 20/40/60-day intervals both ways: 20-day high/low = near-term pool (scalps/day trades), 40-day = short-term, 60-day = intermediate-term.
- Turtle soup origin: Richard Dennis's Turtle traders bought 20-day-high breakouts and sold 20-day-low breakdowns (long-term trend following, like the big FX funds). Those breakouts are often false → the edge. (ICT credits the turtle soup pattern to the book "Street Smarts".)
- BIAS RULE: if buy stops keep getting taken and sell stops rarely → institutional order flow is BULLISH (don't short); the reverse = bearish. When the side being raided flips (e.g. sell stops start getting hit after a run of buy-stop raids), suspect a market structure / QUARTERLY SHIFT. Below the 60-day low = deep discount; above the 60-day high = deep premium.
- Example: CAD futures / USD/CAD, Aug 2016 → Jan 2017, stepping the 60/60 window forward monthly; buy stops above the July highs were raided in September, then the October highs, and so on.
- USD/CAD daily: sweeps below the candle bodies near 1.3050, then above old bodies, then below again — each a fund-level stop raid.
- CAD futures (Nov–Dec 2016): ran sell stops below ~0.7380 at support while OPEN INTEREST collapsed (a 15% decline is "massive"); smart money unwilling to stay short → ~300-pip rally to ~0.7680.
- Simplest form: keep a rolling 120-day window (60 back, 60 forward); you can execute purely off the daily chart.
