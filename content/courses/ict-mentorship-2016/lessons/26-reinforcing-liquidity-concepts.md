- EXTERNAL range liquidity: buy-side above the current range high, sell-side below the range low (liquidity pools). Runs on it can be low- or high-resistance — trade the low-resistance ones.
- INTERNAL range liquidity: while the range holds, liquidity voids and fair value gaps fill ("gap risk" — the market reprices quickly to where little trading happened); order blocks inside the range get new buys/sells; market maker buy/sell models form inside ranges.
- ICT's core habit: ENTER at internal range liquidity (an OB/FVG inside the range), EXIT at external range liquidity (stops beyond the range high/low). Each new range = previous high to new low (or vice versa); what's "external" on the daily can still be "internal" on the monthly.
- USD/JPY 2016 example: monthly — price ran an old high, retraced, cleared equal lows; then targets inside the range: up-candle open, a monthly FVG (~114.55–115.95) and up to the bearish OB low ~118.85; all-time context high 125.77. With the monthly bullish, every daily/4-hour/15-minute high was easily run (low-resistance), and buys at prior bullish OBs were repeatedly re-used (smart money's average ~113.25–113.50) until price hit the monthly FVG — heavy profit-taking there. ICT stayed out the first two weeks of November (US election).
- "One setup per week is all you're looking for."
- If the market is retracing against the HTF bias, look for the lows it will breach (sell stops), want a QUICK reaction away, then buy the next bullish OB.
- SWING SIZE FILTER: on 15-minute/hourly charts, skip setups where the distance from the OB entry to the range high is only ~20 pips; take ~40+ pips; for 50-pip goals use 30/60-minute charts and look for 75–80+ pip ranges (you don't need the range to break to profit). For ~100 pips a week, trade 4-hour/1-hour charts across the majors. For 250 pips over two weeks, use 4-hour/daily.
- Trades against the HTF bias are high-resistance: they stall, don't pay, or stop you out.
