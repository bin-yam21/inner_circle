- Start the macro view with the central bank rate table (e.g. from FXStreet). Money seeks yield; funds buy high-yield currencies against low-yield ones.
- Process: (1) pick a high-rate country and a low-rate one (a strong difference, not necessarily the extremes) → pair them; (2) find strong higher-timeframe support (to buy) or resistance (to sell); (3) wait for smart-money clues — seasonal tendency and/or open interest (one or two are enough); (4) confirm direction with the US Dollar Index.
- AUD/USD example (late 2016): RBA 1.5% vs Fed 0.50% (0.75% after the December 2016 hike). Long-term old low 0.7150 as support; March 2017 AUD futures showed a sharp DROP in open interest (commercial short covering) → 400+ pip rally from 0.7150; DXY made a higher high while AUD failed to make a lower low (SMT).
- USD/JPY example: Fed 0.50% vs BoJ negative → buy USD/JPY (sell yen). Yen futures hit a weekly bearish OB (near the 98 big figure) and fell 1,200+ pips around the Trump election.
- Homework: look back 3–6 months for pairs with large rate differentials and justify their big moves using HTF levels, order flow and open interest. Avoid exotic pairs.
