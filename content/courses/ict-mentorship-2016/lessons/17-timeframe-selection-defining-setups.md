- Timeframes by style:
  - MONTHLY → position trading (good for "intraday-intolerant" traders; also the source of ICT's own precision).
  - WEEKLY → swing trading (1–2 trades per quarter).
  - DAILY → short-term trading; ICT's favourite chart ("if someone held a gun to my head") — long-term view plus all the near-term bank and fund levels and the daily bias.
  - 4-HOUR or less → day trading (exits by 2 p.m. New York).
  - Short-term trades last ~1–5 days. ICT isn't a position trader (too impatient, wants "velocity" of money); he's a short-term/day trader.
- Find YOUR model: your patience, temperament and life (job, family, study) must fit the timeframe; one "bread and butter" setup works on every timeframe. Start with the daily chart even if you want to day trade.
- Styles: trend trader (only in the monthly/weekly direction, long holds); swing trader (daily intermediate swings); contrarian (reversal patterns at market extremes/capitulation, e.g. above a previous month's high); short-term; day trader.
- EUR/USD MONTHLY case (2008–2010): consolidation ~1.5100–1.5200 → high ~1.5950 → collapse to ~1.2450 (thousands of pips in ~4 months). Your eye goes to the LAST UP CANDLE before the drop (monthly bearish OB). Over a year later price returned to it (~1.5080); below lay "too clean" equal lows/bodies near 1.2200 = large FUND sell stops (trend-following funds leave stops in for a long time). Took 13 months to set up, ~6 months to unfold, ~2,900 pips.
- Breakdown: weekly → sell breakers and bearish OBs in the monthly direction; daily → put a Fib on the known range (monthly OB to the target lows) and expect setups near the 25%, 50%, 75% grades.
- Daily setups inside the bearish monthly range: turtle soups (runs just above short-term highs, then acceleration lower), breakers, OTE (79%), bearish OBs.
- ICT trades ONLY THREE setups: (1) trading inside the range (pullback into an exposed range/void, then expansion), (2) bearish/bullish order blocks, (3) stop runs / turtle soups above old highs or below old lows. "I could just reduce it to one." Pick the one you see most easily; don't copy friends.
