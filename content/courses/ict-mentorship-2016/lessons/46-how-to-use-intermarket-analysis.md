- Four groups: bonds/interest rates, commodities, stocks, currencies. They lead and lag each other (often 6–12 months on long-term trends), not lockstep.
- Bonds and stocks move TOGETHER (rising bond prices support stocks; falling bonds = rising yields weigh on stocks). The T-bond leads stocks. Exception: deflation (e.g. late 1998) — bonds up, stocks and commodities down.
- Commodities move OPPOSITE to bond prices (commodities lead inflation). Measure with the CRB index (grain/livestock-heavy), the Goldman Sachs Commodity Index (energy) and the GS Industrial Metals index (copper, zinc, tin, aluminium — global demand).
- US Dollar Index is INVERSE to commodities; a strong dollar hurts grain/meat exports. Dollar up → commodity currencies down.
- Key pairs: DXY bullish → gold bearish; gold bullish → AUD and NZD bullish (gold exporters); oil bullish → USD/CAD bearish; gold bearish → USD/CAD bullish; Dow up ↔ Nikkei up; Nikkei down → USD/JPY bearish; yields down → that currency bearish (money seeks yield).
- Use it as confirmation for long-term analysis: if many relationships agree, your bias is probably right. Higher-timeframe entries need wider stops and patience.
- Philosophy: prices of these markets already reflect the fundamentals that institutions' trained staff analyse, so you don't need to read all the economic data.
