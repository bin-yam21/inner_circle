- Slide A, False Bull Flags: (1) not every sudden rally into a short-term consolidation is a bull flag; (2) in MATURE bull trends or at HIGHER-TIMEFRAME DISTRIBUTION levels, price will post false bull flags; (3) retail sees a classic "continuation buy pattern", but it results in a reversal; (4) higher-timeframe charts and PREMIUM markets help identify them.
- Slide B, False Bear Flags: the mirror — in mature bear trends or at HTF ACCUMULATION levels; retail sees a "continuation sell pattern" that reverses; use HTF charts and DISCOUNT markets.
- Shows the textbook flag (flag pole, flag channel, target = pole height projected from the breakout), then real examples.
- Example 1, AUD/USD (19–20 Oct 2016, H1/M5): a sharp spike up into a red higher-timeframe premium zone (~0.7660–0.7740, around the OTE/79% levels near 0.7700–0.7705) formed "a classic bull flag". Instead of continuing, price sold off from the flag through equilibrium to ~0.7600 and below over the following days.
- Example 2, AUD/USD (Sept 2016): a daily bullish order block / discount zone (blue, ~0.7440–0.7460, from July 2016) was retested in mid-September; on the hourly, the decline into it looked like a bear flag, but price held the zone and reversed higher.
- Takeaway: judge a flag by WHERE it forms (premium vs discount, HTF PD arrays), not by its shape.
