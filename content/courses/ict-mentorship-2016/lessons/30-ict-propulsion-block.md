- Definition: a candle that has already traded down into a down-candle / bullish order block, and then takes over the role of support for higher prices. "A down closed candle that trades down into a previous down closed candle."
- It is highly sensitive: the mean threshold (half of the body) should never break. Usually price only trades down into the propulsion candle's HIGH and explodes away — "sudden and violent movement".
- Several consecutive down candles = treat as one bullish order block; confirmation when price trades through the highest of those down candles.
- Example: 15-minute chart, entry at 133.45 where the open and the low of the retesting candle met the propulsion candle.
- Rule: if the mean threshold breaks, it's probably not a good trade → you can use an ultra-tight stop, and failure gives immediate feedback (go flat or look for a reversal).
- Bearish version: last up candle (bearish OB) → price returns, the new up candle trading into it is the propulsion candle; price only needs to reach the propulsion candle's LOW to sell. "Very little drawdown, immediate responsiveness."
