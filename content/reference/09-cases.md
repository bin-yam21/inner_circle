<p>These are real 2026 events from published sources, with rounded figures, followed by the lesson rules that apply. Treat each one as an exercise. They describe what happened, not what will happen next.</p>

<div class="case">
<p class="when">Fed · US 10-year · Dollar Index · Sept–Oct 2026</p>
<h3>The first Fed hike in three years and a 24-year high in yields</h3>
<div class="cols">
<div>
<h4>What happened</h4>
<ul>
<li>16 Sept 2026: the FOMC raised rates 25 bp to 3.75–4.00%.</li>
<li>The US 10-year yield rose ~0.54 points in a month to ~5.33–5.35% (6–7 Oct), the highest since 2002. Drivers: oil and tanker attacks in Hormuz, deficits, and heavy issuance.</li>
<li>DXY rose ~2% in September to above 101, a two-month high (~101.5).</li>
<li>EUR/USD fell from its ~1.1700 August high to ~1.1375 (28 Sept).</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="lens">
<h4>Which lessons apply</h4>
<ul>
<li><b>M5 10-Year Notes.</b> Note prices fell (yields rose) while DXY rose, so they moved in opposite directions. ICT calls that a trending environment for the dollar, not a range.</li>
<li><b>M5 Qualifying Conditions.</b> Watch for a "crack in correlation". Yields made a 24-year high, but DXY only made a two-month high. Is the dollar confirming the move, or is this a divergence?</li>
<li><b>M4 Rate Effects.</b> Before trading a DXY level, check the 30/10/5-year triad for a failure swing.</li>
<li><b>Seasonal context.</b> The 10-year seasonal is bullish for notes into December, and 2026 is doing the opposite. ICT's rule: when the strongest seasonal fails, lean the other way. DXY's seasonal decline into late Oct/early Nov, however, conflicts with the hikes.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<p><b>Exercise.</b> Anchor on 1 Sept 2026, mark the EUR/USD 20/40/60-day lows and highs, and cast forward 60 days. Is sell-side liquidity still being raided? Where is the nearest bearish OB or FVG that price would retrace into?</p>
</div>

<div class="case">
<p class="when">Gold · Jan → Sept 2026</p>
<h3>Record high, a 14% quarter, central-bank buying</h3>
<div class="cols">
<div>
<h4>What happened</h4>
<ul>
<li>Gold hit an all-time high of ~$5,500–5,600 on 28–29 Jan 2026.</li>
<li>March brought a deleveraging sell-off. Q2 fell ~14%, the largest quarterly drop in a decade, to a low near $3,955–3,995 in late June.</li>
<li>Central banks bought a record ~289 t in Q2. Gold was ~$4,340–4,370 by mid-September.</li>
</ul>
</div>
<div class="lens">
<h4>Which lessons apply</h4>
<ul>
<li><b>M1 Liquidity Runs and the turtle soup.</b> An all-time high sits on buy-stop liquidity, which is where distribution happens.</li>
<li><b>M5 Quarterly Shifts.</b> Jan high to late-June low is roughly a 3–6 month swing. Compare the 60-day look-back from 1 June.</li>
<li><b>M3 Institutional Sponsorship and the COT lessons.</b> Record central-bank buying into the decline looks like "smart money" accumulating in discount.</li>
<li><b>M5 Intermarket.</b> ICT pairs gold with DXY (inverse) and with AUD/NZD (gold exporters). Check whether AUD and NZD confirmed gold's turn.</li>
</ul>
</div>
</div>
<p><b>Exercise.</b> On the weekly chart, mark the Jan high, the June low and their 50% (~$4,750). Is mid-September price in discount? Which premium array would ICT target first, following the hierarchy order?</p>
</div>

<div class="case">
<p class="when">Seasonal watchlist · October–December 2026</p>
<h3>What ICT's seasonal tables say to watch now</h3>
<ul>
<li><b>NZD/USD:</b> a Sept–Oct seasonal low, then a late-Nov/early-Dec buy tendency. Valid only if NZD structure is bullish.</li>
<li><b>USD/CAD:</b> CAD futures tend to weaken from September until Christmas, which favours USD/CAD, if the pair is already bullish. Watch oil, since oil up means USD/CAD down.</li>
<li><b>Dollar Index:</b> a seasonal tendency to decline into the last week of October / first week of November, then a November bounce. Weigh this against the Fed hikes and rising yields.</li>
</ul>
<p>ICT's caveat, from every seasonal lesson: "a roadmap, not a panacea". Seasonals need technical confirmation and are overridden by macro shocks, as in 2008.</p>
