<p>These are teaching diagrams with illustrative prices, built to match ICT's definitions. Green candles closed up; red candles closed down.</p>

<h3>1. Impulse swing, equilibrium and OTE</h3>
<figure class="fig">
  <div><div class="chart"><svg id="c1" viewBox="0 0 560 300" role="img" aria-label="Bullish impulse swing with premium, discount and OTE zones"></svg></div><figcaption>M1 · Equilibrium vs Discount · Equilibrium vs Premium</figcaption></div>
  <div class="txt">
    <p>Measure an impulsive price swing from its low to its high. The swing high needs a lower candle on each side, and a fourth candle must close lower before you start watching for a pullback.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>At 50% (equilibrium), price is at fair value: neutral to bullish.</li>
      <li>Below 50% is <b>discount</b>. The highest-probability buys are in the 62–79% OTE zone; the sweet spot is 70.5%.</li>
      <li>In a bullish market, discount prices "don't stay long". Expect a fast move away.</li>
      <li>If price breaks the OTE and takes an old low, look for a <b>turtle soup</b> long, not a breakdown.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</figure>

<h3>2. Liquidity raid and market structure shift</h3>
<figure class="fig">
  <div><div class="chart"><svg id="c2" viewBox="0 0 560 300" role="img" aria-label="Sell-side liquidity sweep below equal lows then structure shift"></svg></div><figcaption>M1 Liquidity Runs · M4 Liquidity Pools · M4 Double Bottom</figcaption></div>
  <div class="txt">
    <p>Equal lows are "too clean". Sell stops pile up below them.</p>
    <ul>
      <li>On 15/30-minute charts, expect a 10–20 pip sweep below the low.</li>
      <li>ICT's limit entry goes 10–20 pips under the low with a 30–50 pip stop.</li>
      <li>If price runs more than ~25 pips past the low, it is probably a real decline, not a raid.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>Confirmation is a displacement through the last short-term high.</p>
  </div>
</figure>

<h3>3. Order block, mean threshold and FVG</h3>
<figure class="fig">
  <div><div class="chart"><svg id="c3" viewBox="0 0 560 300" role="img" aria-label="Bullish order block with mean threshold and fair value gap"></svg></div><figcaption>M4 Orderblocks · M4 Fair Value Gaps · M4 Propulsion Block</figcaption></div>
  <div class="txt">
    <p>A <b>bullish OB</b> is the lowest down-close candle with the largest body, near support. It is confirmed when a later candle trades through its high.</p>
    <p>Trade it this way:</p>
    <ul>
      <li>Entry: on the return to the OB's open/high, plus ~5 pips for the spread.</li>
      <li>Stop: below the OB low, or just under the mean threshold.</li>
      <li>Patience: ICT prefers to see a rally of 2–3× the OB's body height before expecting a valid pullback.</li>
    </ul>
    <p>A <b>fair value gap</b> spans the left candle's low to the right candle's high (bearish case). Only one side of the market traded inside it, so price usually comes back to rebalance it.</p>
  </div>
</figure>

<h3>4. Breaker</h3>
<figure class="fig">
  <div><div class="chart"><svg id="c4" viewBox="0 0 560 300" role="img" aria-label="Bullish breaker after a lower low and break of the swing high"></svg></div><figcaption>M4 ICT Breaker Block · M5 Institutional Swing Points</figcaption></div>
  <div class="txt">
    <p>A bullish breaker forms in three steps:</p>
    <ol>
      <li>Price makes a short-term low, then a lower low that runs the sell stops.</li>
      <li>Price breaks the swing high between the two lows.</li>
      <li>The up-close candle range in that swing high now becomes support.</li>
    </ol>
    <p>Why it works: the sellers who pushed price below the old low are now underwater. They buy back at break-even, and smart money adds longs at the same level.</p>
    <p>ICT calls the breaker swing point "the most powerful, the most dynamic" pattern.</p>
  </div>
</figure>
