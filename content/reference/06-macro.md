<h3>Interest rates (M3 Macro→Micro, M4 Rate Effects, M5 lessons 2.1–2.3)</h3>
<ul>
<li>Bond prices move opposite to yields. Falling bonds mean higher yields, which supports the dollar.</li>
<li><b>Interest-rate triad:</b> the 30-year bond, 10-year note and 5-year note futures (free on barchart.com). When DXY reaches a key PD array, a failure swing in any one of the three validates the setup. If there's no sign of smart money, skip the trade.</li>
<li><b>10-year note vs DXY.</b>
  <ul>
    <li>Notes following their seasonal while DXY moves the opposite way means a trending environment, good for position trades.</li>
    <li>Notes and DXY moving in the <i>same</i> direction means indecision and a big consolidation, so trade short-term only.</li>
  </ul>
</li>
<li><b>Rate differentials.</b> Pair a high-yield currency with a low-yield one, then wait for a higher-timeframe level plus open interest or a seasonal, and confirm with DXY.
  <ul>
    <li>AUD/USD: RBA 1.5% vs Fed 0.5–0.75%, a 0.7150 support and collapsing open interest gave a 400+ pip rally.</li>
    <li>USD/JPY: a rising Fed rate against the BoJ's negative rate gave a 1,200+ pip move.</li>
  </ul>
</li>
</ul>
<h3>Intermarket relationships (M5 lesson 3)</h3>
<div class="tw"><table>
<thead><tr><th>Relationship</th><th>Direction</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Bonds ↔ stocks</td><td>Move together; bonds lead by 6–12 months (except in deflation)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Bonds ↔ commodities</td><td>Inverse; commodities lead inflation (CRB, GSCI energy, GS industrial metals)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Dollar Index ↔ commodities</td><td>Inverse</td></tr>
<tr><td>DXY bullish → gold</td><td>Bearish</td></tr>
<tr><td>Gold bullish → AUD, NZD</td><td>Bullish (gold exporters)</td></tr>
<tr><td>Oil bullish → USD/CAD</td><td>Bearish</td></tr>
<tr><td>Dow ↔ Nikkei</td><td>Together; Nikkei down → USD/JPY down</td></tr>
<tr><td>Yields down → that currency</td><td>Bearish: "money seeks yield"</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>
<h3>Seasonal tendencies (M5 lessons 2.1, 4.1–4.3)</h3>
<p>These come from 40-year and 15-year (NZD: 19-year) futures averages. Only use one when the market is already leaning that way. Remember to flip the futures seasonal for USD/xxx pairs.</p>
<div class="tw"><table>
<thead><tr><th>Market</th><th>Tendency</th></tr></thead>
<tbody>
<tr><td>Dollar Index</td><td>Rallies Jan–Feb; high around Mar–Apr then a low in May; major high June–July; declines into late Oct/early Nov; small bounce in November</td></tr>
<tr><td>10-year T-note</td><td>High Jan–Feb → low late May–early July → rally into December</td></tr>
<tr><td>AUD/USD</td><td>Rallies March → top in May</td></tr>
<tr><td>NZD/USD</td><td>Lows Mar–Apr, Jun–Jul, Sep–Oct, late Nov; sell-offs mid-Feb→mid-Mar, May→Jun/Jul, mid-Aug→Oct</td></tr>
<tr><td>GBP/USD</td><td>Low in March → high in May</td></tr>
<tr><td>EUR/USD</td><td>Seasonal low June–July</td></tr>
<tr><td>USD/CHF</td><td>DXY high and CHF low June–July</td></tr>
<tr><td>USD/JPY</td><td>Yen low Mar–Apr; sell USD/JPY then if the dollar is bearish or the yen bullish</td></tr>
<tr><td>USD/CAD</td><td>CAD futures weak Sept → Christmas, so USD/CAD bullish; CAD strong mid-Mar → May/June. Crude oil rallies ~Mar → June</td></tr>
</tbody></table></div>
