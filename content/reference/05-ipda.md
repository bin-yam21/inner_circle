<p>From the January 2017 lessons 1.1–1.5.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Anchor.</b> Draw a vertical line on the first trading day of the most recent completed month. If the date is 14 November, use 1 October.</li>
<li><b>Look back</b> 20, 40 and 60 trading days. Ask:
  <ul>
    <li>Was order flow up or down?</li>
    <li>Where are the significant high and low (the liquidity)?</li>
    <li>Where are the OBs, FVGs, voids and rejection blocks?</li>
  </ul>
  Re-anchor to the obvious intermediate high or low that marks the last structure shift.</li>
<li><b>Cast forward</b> 20, 40 and 60 trading days. The next setup or shift is expected inside that window.</li>
<li><b>Open float</b> is the rolling 120-day window (60 back, 60 forward). Its highest high and lowest low are where the large funds' stops sit. The 20-day range is near-term (day trades), 40-day is short-term, and 60-day is intermediate.</li>
<li><b>Bias rule.</b> If buy stops keep getting raided and sell stops rarely do, institutional order flow is bullish, so don't short. When the side being raided flips, expect a quarterly shift.</li>
<li><b>Accumulation and distribution versus the Dollar Index</b> (daily chart):
  <ul>
    <li>DXY makes a lower low while EUR/USD makes a lower high: distribution in the euro.</li>
    <li>DXY makes a higher high while EUR/USD makes a higher low: accumulation in the euro.</li>
  </ul>
  USD/CHF, USD/JPY and USD/CAD move with DXY; EUR/USD and GBP/USD move against it.</li>
<li><b>Swing hierarchy</b> (from Larry Williams): a short-term high is a candle with lower highs on both sides. An intermediate-term high has lower short-term highs on both sides. A long-term high has lower intermediate-term highs on both sides.</li>
</ul>
